Talaria Technologies · AI Operations

Design the ceiling.
Then remove what you don't need.

A top-down prompt architecture for company-wide AI adoption

13 September 2026
Open on the contrast, not the agenda. Most people build prompts upward from nothing. We build downward from a complete specification. Everything in this deck follows from that one reversal.
The prompting spectrum

Two directions of travel

Status quo · Undershooting

Start vague.
Build up.

  • 1Vague opening prompt
  • 2Inductive patching, turn by turn
  • 3Context drift
  • 4Rework and wasted turns
Strategic · Deductive

Start complete.
Cut down.

  • 1High-density master prompt
  • 2Lock the constraints
  • 3Distil to the task
  • 4Controlled execution
Same model, same person, opposite direction. Undershooting negotiates with the model. Deductive prompting instructs it. Ask the room which one they did this morning.
Why undershooting fails

Every correction adds a contradiction

  • Each patch is written against the last output, not the original intent.
  • Constraints added late silently overwrite constraints added early.
The failure is structural, not a skill gap. By turn six nobody in the thread can say what the governing constraint actually is — including the model.
Deductive prompting

Overshoot, then subtract

01

Overshoot

Write the master prompt: role, audience, constraints, format, tone, and what to leave out.

02

Constrain

Mark what must not change. These survive every derivative prompt.

03

Distil

Strip everything this task does not need. Subtraction is cheap; recovery is not.

04

Execute

One pass. Review against the master, not against the last reply.

Overshooting control is the point. It is far easier to delete a constraint you wrote deliberately than to discover one you never set.
Case study · Talaria credit facility

One context load, three deliverables

The master prompt carried

  • Company, product and business model
  • Audience: a risk-averse credit reader
  • The ask, the leverage, the concessions
  • Tone, length, and named omissions

What came out

  • Three letter variants — concise, expanded, full-block
  • Three formatted Word documents
  • A slide deck, from the same context
  • The argument was never rebuilt.
This is our own evidence, not a vendor case study. The format changed three times. The reasoning was authored once, because it was specified before the first draft existed.
Operational impact

Control without a bottleneck

01

Responsibility

Standardised inputs make output governable. What the model was told is auditable after the fact.

02

Empowerment

Teams inherit the context. No one needs to be a prompt engineer to get a compliant result.

03

Flexibility

Departments tune parameters, not principles. The core constraints hold across every unit.

The usual trade-off is control versus speed. A master prompt removes it: the control lives in the artefact, so the team does not have to carry it.

Strategic alignment

The engine sits at the centre

Centre-aligned, not top-down in the org-chart sense. Every unit is the same distance from the core, so no team inherits a degraded copy of the standard.
Adoption model

Govern the core, not the forks

PHASE 1

Author

One master prompt per work category, owned centrally and version-controlled.

PHASE 2

Fork

Units distil their own variants. Parameters are theirs; constraints are not.

PHASE 3

Review

Audit outputs against the master. Amend the core once; every fork inherits it.

Policing individual prompts does not scale. Governing one artefact that every prompt descends from does.
The thesis
Undershooting is not a skill problem.
It is a direction problem.

Specify the ceiling once. Everything after that is subtraction.

Land it and stop. Do not add a summary slide — the sentence is the summary.
