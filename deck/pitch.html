The problem we solve

Flat commissions break remote routes

  • Under a flat commission, a driver earns materially less on a low-density route than on a dense urban one.
  • So drivers decline and cancel those trips.
  • Riders outside the core grid get unreliable service.
  • The platform quietly loses the demand it cannot serve.
The incentive to refuse the hard route is built into the pricing.
This is a structural failure, not an execution failure — that framing matters, because structural problems have structural fixes a lender can underwrite.
The mechanism

A platform fee that moves with route density

Averaging 5% — lower on the routes that are hardest to serve, higher where volume is dense.

Driver take-home is levelled across route types, so the reason to refuse a remote trip is removed at the source.

The curve is illustrative, not fitted data — say so if asked. The point is direction, not slope.
Why this matters to a lender

Stability, not upside

01

Supply is retained

Drivers stay available across route types, which sustains fulfilment.

02

Volume is predictable

Sustained fulfilment means transaction volume does not swing with geography.

03

Margin is stabilised

A fee that floats around 5% rather than spiking produces steady revenue per trip.

A volume-and-reliability model, not a high-variance one — the profile a lender should prefer.

Resist pitching growth here. Bankers underwrite downside. Stability is the stronger claim with this audience.
Status

Phase 1 is complete. Phase 2 is compute.

Phase 1 — done

Core software architecture finalised. Facility used as underwritten. Unit economics evaluated and approved by this bank.

Phase 2 — beginning

Parameter training and real-time mapping simulation on cloud GPU banks. Approx. [Insert $/month] across [Insert N] environments.

Emphasise "as underwritten" — the facility was used exactly as modelled. That earns the right to ask for more.
Phase 2 workload

Two jobs, both GPU-bound

  • Parameter training. The dynamic fee is only defensible if it is accurate — trained against historical route density, cancellation behaviour, driver earnings distributions and demand seasonality.
  • Real-time mapping simulation. Pricing logic is validated against simulated network conditions — traffic, surge, supply shocks, edge-case routes — before any driver or rider sees it.
Keep this short. The technical detail is credibility, not the argument. Do not let a banker's eyes glaze here.
The constraint

Transfer velocity, not solvency

  • GPU capacity is procured on demand, billed on short usage-based cycles.
  • Outflows are high-frequency and unevenly distributed — not a smooth monthly draft.
  • Our daily ceiling was set against an even, development-stage burn rate.
  • Training runs now stall waiting on settlement rather than on capital.
The ratios are unchanged. What is misaligned is the shape of the facility against the shape of the spend.
THE critical slide. A transfer-limit complaint can read as a liquidity warning. Name the misreading out loud before they reach it themselves.
A deliberate choice

One account, on purpose

We could relieve this pressure by spreading activity across institutions. We have declined to.

Transparency

One unbroken transaction record, no reconciliation gaps.

Compliance

A single reporting perimeter for tax and regulatory filing.

Auditability

One consolidated ledger rather than parallel histories.

Your credit team keeps continuous visibility into our full cash position. Fragmenting our banking would degrade that for both of us.

Frame consolidation as their benefit, never our convenience. If asked why not a second bank — answer with visibility, not with tax.
What we are prepared to offer

We will share the cost of speed

  • Adjusted maintenance fees or interest margin in exchange for an expedited review.
  • Tiered drawdown — the incremental tranche released against defined Phase 2 milestones.
  • Enhanced reporting — monthly compute reconciliation and updated coverage ratios.
  • Scheduled review at Phase 2 completion, re-sizing the facility against demonstrated results.
Do not name a rate premium out loud. Whatever number you say first becomes the floor of the negotiation.

Phase 1 rested on this bank's willingness to underwrite a model that was then unproven. It is now proven. This request asks only that the facility be kept sized to the business it supports.

Part two

How the argument was built

Shift register here — you stop being the CEO and start being the writer analysing the choices.
Persuasive strategy

Four moves, one audience

01

Continuity over novelty

Framed as scaling an approved plan, not a new ask — lowers perceived risk.

02

Name the misreading

States it is velocity, not solvency, before the reader can conclude otherwise.

03

Reframe self-interest

Consolidation presented as the bank's visibility, not our convenience.

04

Offer constraint

Volunteered safeguards persuade a committee more than a higher rate does.

Each move maps to a specific reader anxiety. Ask the class which anxiety each one answers.
Deliberate omissions

What was left out, and why

  • The threat to move banks. Coercive mid-relationship; triggers committee caution rather than speed. Implied once, softly, instead.
  • A named 15% premium. Stating a number first makes it the floor of the negotiation, not the ceiling.
  • Urgency language. "Detrimental to our progress" reads as distress to a risk-aware reader; the cost of delay is shown concretely instead.
Strongest slide for a writing class — persuasion is as much about restraint as about argument.
