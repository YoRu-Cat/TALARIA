The problem we solve

Flat commissions break remote routes

  • Under a flat commission, a driver earns materially less on a low-density route than on a dense urban one.
  • So drivers decline and cancel those trips.
  • Riders outside the core grid get unreliable service.
  • The platform quietly loses the demand it cannot serve.
The incentive to refuse the hard route is built into the pricing.
The mechanism

A platform fee that moves with route density

Averaging 5% — lower on the routes that are hardest to serve, higher where volume is dense.

Driver take-home is levelled across route types, so the reason to refuse a remote trip is removed at the source.

Why this matters to a lender

Stability, not upside

01

Supply is retained

Drivers stay available across route types, which sustains fulfilment.

02

Volume is predictable

Sustained fulfilment means transaction volume does not swing with geography.

03

Margin is stabilised

A fee that floats around 5% rather than spiking produces steady revenue per trip.

This is a volume-and-reliability model, not a high-variance one — the profile a lender should prefer.

  • Parameter training. The dynamic fee is only defensible if it is accurate — trained against historical route density, cancellation behaviour, driver earnings distributions and demand seasonality.

The constraint

Transfer velocity, not solvency

  • GPU capacity is procured on demand, billed on short usage-based cycles.
  • Outflows are high-frequency and unevenly distributed — not a smooth monthly draft.
  • Our daily ceiling was set against an even, development-stage burn rate.
  • Training runs now stall waiting on settlement rather than on capital.
The ratios are unchanged. What is misaligned is the shape of the facility against the shape of the spend.

Deliberate omissions

What was left out, and why

  • The threat to move banks. Coercive in a mid-relationship letter; tends to trigger committee caution rather than speed. Implied once, softly, instead.
  • A named 15% premium. Stating a specific number first makes it the floor of the negotiation, not the ceiling.
  • Urgency language. "Detrimental to our progress" reads as distress to a risk-aware reader; the cost of delay is shown concretely instead.
